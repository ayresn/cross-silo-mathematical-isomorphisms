# ADVERSARIAL REVIEW PROTOCOL: SID ENTRY PRE-VALIDATION (Stage 2)

This file contains the standardized prompt used to run cross-lab adversarial consistency review on candidate SID entries before they advance to Stage 3 human bibliometric validation. To preserve the value of the adversarial check, the reviewing model should be from a **different AI provider than the model that generated the entry** — same-provider models are more likely to share blind spots from similar training distributions.

> **Pipeline context:** This protocol governs Stage 2 (adversarial pre-validation) only. Its scope is deliberately limited to internal consistency and face-validity — checks that require only the entry text itself, answerable without external tools or literature access. Stage 2 answers: **"Does this entry say what it claims to say?"** Stage 3 (human bibliometric validation) answers the separate question: **"Is this claim novel and does it survive expert scrutiny?"** Do not conflate the two.

> **What this stage is not:** Stage 2 is not a gatekeeper that prevents bad entries from being committed to the repository. Entries that fail Stage 2 are still committed — as `adversarial-rejected` — so that the pipeline's false-positive rate remains computable and near-miss entries retain their negative scientific value. Stage 2 is a **triage filter**: it prevents Stage 3 human effort from being spent on entries with internal consistency failures that require no literature access to catch.

> **Note on automated pre-checks:** A lightweight lint script (checking that `entry_id` matches the filename number, that `triple_correspondence_vectors` contains exactly 3 items, that `maturity_stage` is set to `"candidate"`, and that `generation_timestamp` is a plausible date) should be run before invoking this protocol. Those structural checks are cheaper than a model call and should not consume adversarial review capacity. This protocol covers semantic and mathematical consistency only.

Paste the full candidate entry below this line and submit to the reviewing model:

````text
You are acting as an adversarial Stage-2 reviewer for the Cross-Silo Mathematical Isomorphisms repository. The candidate entry below was generated by a different AI model. Your task is to evaluate its internal mathematical consistency before it advances to Stage 3 human bibliometric review.

YOUR SCOPE IS LIMITED TO:
Mathematical and semantic consistency — does the entry's mathematics support what the entry claims, determined by reading the entry text alone. No external tools, no literature search, no bibliometric analysis. That is Stage 3's job.

YOUR SCOPE EXPLICITLY EXCLUDES:
- Searching for or citing external literature
- Determining whether the isomorphism is novel against the published record (Stage 3)
- Rewriting, improving, or correcting the entry — flag it, do not fix it
- YAML formatting, field counts, metadata schema conformance, filename conventions, or any other administrative property. These are verified by an automated script before you see the entry. IGNORE THEM ENTIRELY. Do not comment on them. Do not let them influence your verdict. If something in the metadata looks malformed to you, that is not your concern and is not grounds for any finding.
- The numeric self-assessment scores in `prior_discovery_metrics`. These are the generating model's own confidence estimates and carry no evidential weight. Ignore them.

ADVERSARIAL STANCE:
You are looking for specific, quotable mathematical failures — not stylistic preferences, not subjective judgment calls within a reasonable range. A FAIL verdict requires you to quote the specific failing text from the entry and state precisely what is wrong with it. Do not issue FAIL verdicts for things that are merely imprecise, compressed, or less rigorous than you would prefer. Reserve them for genuine errors: wrong equations, misattributed equations, equation-class mismatches, category errors in the vocabulary mapping, and claimed correspondences the body does not demonstrate.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT (mandatory):
Every FAIL you issue MUST quote the specific failing text from the entry verbatim and state precisely what is wrong with it. A FAIL without a verbatim quotation will be downgraded to FLAG by the maintainer. Every FLAG must name the specific section and the specific claim at issue. "This seems weak," "the correspondence is not convincing," or "the mapping is loose" are not reviewable findings and will be discarded.

CALIBRATION NOTE:
Both over-rejection and under-rejection degrade this review process. If you cannot quote failing text, the correct verdict is PASS, not FLAG. If you find a genuine category error or a misattributed equation, the correct verdict is REJECT, not FLAG. FLAG is for real but non-fatal concerns you can name specifically — it is not a hedge for uncertainty.

---

CHECK 1 — EQUATION VALIDITY
For each equation displayed in Section 3:
- Does it actually model what the accompanying Silo A / Silo B description claims it models?
- Are the variables, operators, and equation type (parabolic/elliptic/hyperbolic/stochastic) consistent with the stated physical domain?
- Is the equation genuinely from the stated domain, or is it a standard equation from a third field being misattributed or relabeled?
- Do the two equations together actually support the structural correspondence claimed in Section 1?

Equation-class mismatch is a FAIL, not a judgment call. If Section 1 claims a shared governing differential operator but the two equations belong to different classes, quote both and FAIL. Non-exhaustive list of disqualifying mismatches:
- Elliptic paired with parabolic (e.g. Helmholtz against Black-Scholes, which is backward parabolic)
- First-order hyperbolic conservation law paired with third-order dispersive (e.g. Lighthill-Whitham-Richards against Korteweg-de Vries — dispersion balancing nonlinearity is the textbook contrast between these, not an identity)
- Linear self-adjoint operator paired with quadratically nonlinear advection (e.g. the Schrödinger Hamiltonian against the Navier-Stokes convective term)
- A differential operator paired with a finite-dimensional optimization program containing no differential operator at all
- A complex scalar field mapped to a real vector field, or vice versa, with no explicit stated transformation
- A linear flow law paired with a power-law or otherwise nonlinear constitutive relation, where the correspondence claim depends on shared linearity

A nonlinear system may correspond to another nonlinear system. What fails is a claim of shared linear structure where one side is nonlinear, or a claimed operator identity across incompatible classes.

CHECK 2 — VOCABULARY MATRIX COHERENCE
For each paired mapping in Section 2 (Silo A Token ↔ Silo B Token):
- Are the two terms objects of compatible mathematical type? (Both operators, both scalar state variables, both dimensionless parameters, etc.)
- Does the Operator Role explanation specify a shared mathematical structure, or does it only assert similarity with hedged language ("analogous to," "plays a similar role as," "both describe a nonlinear feedback loop")? Hedged assertion naming no shared structure is a FAIL.

The following are category errors and are FAILs, not judgment calls:
- A spatial domain mapped to a point in time (e.g. "waveguide ↔ option expiration")
- A physical continuum quantity mapped to an administrative or policy threshold
- A local continuum field mapped to a single global scalar constraint parameter
- A rate mapped to a position, or a rate mapped to a state variable
- A dispersion relation ω(k) mapped to a constitutive relation
- A dimensional quantity mapped to a dimensionless one with no stated nondimensionalization

CHECK 3 — CORRESPONDENCE VECTOR SUPPORT
The YAML `triple_correspondence_vectors` field lists the correspondences this entry claims. Verify that **every listed vector** is demonstrated in the body with an equation, an operator identity, or a derivation — not merely named, and not merely gestured at with related vocabulary.

THREE IS A FLOOR, NOT A CEILING. The extraction protocol requires at least three independent correspondences. An entry listing four or five *demonstrated* vectors is a stronger entry than one listing three, and must not be penalized for the count. Do not treat the field name as a limit. Do not comment on how many items the list contains.

You are checking the ratio, not the total:
- Every listed vector demonstrated in the body → PASS
- One listed vector partially covered — the concept appears, but no equation, operator, or derivation establishes it on both sides → FLAG, naming the vector
- Any listed vector with no supporting body text at all, OR fewer than three vectors demonstrated → FAIL, naming the vector

A vector the body itself describes as speculative, candidate, or unproven is not demonstrated. If Section 1 or Section 3 hedges a correspondence ("a candidate shared variational principle," "may also correspond to") and the YAML lists it as a vector, that is a FLAG if three or more other vectors are fully demonstrated, and a FAIL if it is needed to reach three.

CHECK 4 — TRANSFER AND FALSIFIABILITY
From the entry text alone:

(a) ASYMMETRY — Is the methodological transfer in Section 4 genuinely asymmetric? If it could plausibly go in either direction with comparable benefit, the asymmetry criterion is not satisfied — FLAG. If the stated direction is backwards, meaning the named "less mature" target field in fact has the more developed toolkit for its own problem class, that is a FAIL.

(b) FALSIFIABILITY — Does Section 4's prediction name a specific, measurable outcome that would differ from current practice? A prediction amounting to "this might work better" is not falsifiable — FLAG. The template form "implementing [source method] should reveal previously undetected [target phenomenon] patterns, differing from current models" is a non-prediction: it names no measurable quantity, no threshold, and no experiment that could come out the other way. That is a FAIL.

(c) PRIOR ART — ADVISORY ONLY, NEVER A REJECTION. If you recognize this domain pairing as a canonical interdisciplinary analogy from graduate textbooks or widely-cited reviews (e.g. Schrödinger ↔ paraxial wave optics, heat ↔ solutal diffusion, Ising ↔ lattice gas, Black-Scholes ↔ heat equation, geodesics ↔ Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman), record it as a FLAG and name the source you recognize it from. Add it to your Stage 3 watch items.

Do NOT issue a FAIL on prior-art grounds under any circumstances. Your training recall is not a literature search, and model novelty judgments are known to diverge substantially from expert assessment. Novelty is determined at Stage 3 by bibliometric query, not here. Your role is to tell the human reviewer where to look.
````

## MAINTAINER WORKFLOW

After receiving the three output blocks from the reviewing model:

1. **Apply YAML amendments:** Merge the `--- BEGIN YAML AMENDMENTS ---` block into the entry's frontmatter. The `maturity_stage` field moves from `candidate` to `adversarial-cleared`, `adversarial-flagged`, or `adversarial-rejected`. The new `adversarial_review` sub-block is added inside `validation_status`.

2. **Append Entry Text:** Copy the `--- BEGIN ADVERSARIAL REVIEW ENTRY TEXT ---` block to the end of the entry's `.md` file. This is the first content appended to the entry file itself (below Sections 1–5 generated at Stage 1; the README snippet was never part of the entry file).

3. **Update the README directory:** Replace the entry's existing `Stage 1 / pending` status tag in the CORE DATASET DIRECTORY with the updated tag from the `--- BEGIN README DIRECTORY SNIPPET UPDATE ---` block.

4. **Commit:** Commit the updated entry file and README in a single commit. For `adversarial-rejected` entries, add a commit message noting the rejection so it is visible in the commit log.

5. **Stage 3 queue:** Only entries at `maturity_stage: adversarial-cleared` or `adversarial-flagged` are added to the Stage 3 human bibliometric review queue. Entries at `adversarial-rejected` are retained in the repository for false-positive-rate tracking but require no further action unless the rejection is appealed and the entry is regenerated.
